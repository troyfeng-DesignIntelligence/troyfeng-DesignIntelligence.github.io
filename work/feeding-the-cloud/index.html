Feeding the Cloud

A critical wearable. Each food-related upload, a photo, a search, a booking, an order, fills a second stomach a little, so the feeding of the cloud can be felt, seen and argued with.

Many of my mealspass through apps, and each photo, search, booking and order is stored elsewhere. The data grows while my body stays the same, and I never decided to feed it. Feeding the Cloud is a critical wearable: a second stomach that fills a little with each food-related upload, so this feeding can be felt, seen and argued with.

01The diary

Four diary entries, an app in each.

For the Self Data brief I kept a handwritten food diary. In four entries, an app stood between me and the meal.

02The film

I didn’t eat more. Something else did.

The project film, 2:30, follows an ordinary food day and ends on a cloud at the table. The cloud is an AI-generated shot; the rest is filmed.

03The concept

Whatever reaches the cloud fills a second stomach.

Design question: how can food data be felt and seen without changing the routines? Below, one action is followed from the phone to the body; each layer carries one decision.

An existing habit is the trigger. Anything that sends food data to the cloud counts, from a post to a few seconds of looking. Feeding it on purpose would make it a game.

Each action adds its own amount of air.

  • Instagram+30
  • TheFork+25
  • Google Maps+15
  • Uber Eats+10
  • looking, scrolling+1–2

the prototype’s scores per action · not measured air

04The show

One wearer felt data had weight.

At the 2026 show, Algorithmic Intimacy in Warfare Times, visitors scanned a QR code and fed it from their own phones. Three remarks stayed with me.

recalled, paraphrased with permission · not a study

  • Mexico, wore itSaid you feel yourself slowly getting full when you eat; wearing it, they felt data’s weight and volume for the first time.
  • Norway, friendWondered what all this data ends up feeding.
  • Italy, classmateKnew Uber Eats learns their taste, but had never seen an Instagram food post as feeding the cloud.

→ my reading A balloon only says “more”, not what a post hands over.

05Data Skin

Data Skin names what a meal becomes.

Self-initiated after the exchange, 2026. The input is the same. Over the bladder sits a gridded skin; as air comes in, the grid shifts against four fixed tags: IMAGE, LOCATION, TIME, CHOICE.

  • bladderan airtight TPU bladder, light blue: it receives
  • grid skina soft TPU skin with a printed grid: it changes
  • PET tagsfour fixed index tags at the edges: they describe
  • orange cordties the skin’s edges to the backing and keeps them visible
  • backingdark fabric with the strap: it carries the wearing

Next, not run: a diary study with wearers. Does a post start to feel like an order?

What this record can't say

  • The scores per action are the web prototype’s own numbers; no air volume was measured, and nothing was counted at the show.
  • The three remarks are recalled months later and paraphrased; they are not a study.
  • The booklet’s data panels show invented figures; they are the book’s concept language, not records.
  • Data Skin is a built object shown through AI-enhanced visuals and concept renders; its diary study with wearers is planned, not run.
  • The studio photographs of the first version were retouched with AI; the process and show photographs were not.

AI use

Parts of the code of the web prototype were written with AI coding assistants. The cloud shots in the film are AI-generated, the studio photographs of the first version were retouched with an AI image tool, and the Data Skin visuals and concept renders were made or enhanced with AI image tools.
