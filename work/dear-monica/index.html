An animated short, 2024

Dear Monica

A letter to my mother, written on screen by a mouse who lives in Shanghai. Every line says that things are fine; the pictures say what the letter leaves out. One minute thirty-eight, drawn mostly frame by frame.

01The letter

Twenty-one lines, and every one of them says that things are fine

The film has no narration but the letter itself, written on screen as the mouse writes it. Click a line to jump to it in the film.

Then five titles, over the desk and the skyline · · · ·

02The mouse

I chose a mouse not only for its vulnerability, but also for its persistence and quiet intelligence.

In the film I portray myself as a mouse: an overlooked, cautious creature surviving in the vast, indifferent city of Shanghai. The mouse is a way to make fragility and powerlessness visible, and the film is a personal expression of generational disconnection as much as a reflection on the pressure of surviving in a city today.

03The city

Three kinds of tower

To build the setting I drew on the dense residential blocks of Beijing’s Pixel complex, the corporate skyline of Lujiazui in Shanghai, and the lone verticality of medieval towers. Together they stand for compression, surveillance and emotional distance, which is how the protagonist’s inner world feels.

  • The Pixel complex, BeijingModular density and anonymity: stacked, repeated windows, a trapped routine, depersonalised living.
  • Lujiazui, ShanghaiAmbition, pressure and the scale of urban capital: the skyline dominates while the individual feels ever smaller.
  • Medieval towersSolitary and fortified: emotional defence and inner distance, verticality as a metaphor for internalised strength and isolation.

Paintings pinned while the settings were drawn: a dome, a tower, a city in fog, a room, a nave. Two more references, Edward Hopper’s Rooms by the Sea and Joan Miró’s Harlequin’s Carnival, are still in copyright and are named, not shown.

04Frame by frame

What the film is made of

Ink drawings animated frame by frame, the city’s photographs drawn over, a 3D stage for the drawn mouse to walk on, and, near the end, the desk the film was made at. Twelve frames, in order.

What this record can't say

  • The letter is the whole script. Nothing is on record about where the film was shown or how it was received, beyond its being on YouTube.
  • The drawn frames exist only inside the film. The storyboard survives as five small images, and there is no count of the frames drawn.
  • The music is two existing songs, credited in the film; this page claims no rights to them. Some backgrounds use footage that is not mine, and the film credits photographs and sounds from friends.
  • The English subtitles are a translation made for this page; the film’s own lines are Chinese.
  • Two of the references, Hopper’s Rooms by the Sea and Miró’s Harlequin’s Carnival, are named but not reproduced, because they are still in copyright.

AI use

A few seconds of the city in motion near the end were generated with Runway Gen-2 from my own skyline composite; the fireworks over it are drawn by hand. The English subtitles on this page were translated with AI assistance.
