person — data and AI

Zingaline

An installation: one visitor at a time, alone at a desk for six to nineteen minutes. You write down one thing you wish you could do over, and the page is shredded. A version of you made from those words takes a son’s place on the night before he leaves home. That night had been rehearsed 2,000 times before you came.

Background

It has never been easier to connect online, but more and more people feel lonely and are afraid to talk face to face. Before an important conversation, many people rehearse it in private first. Large models and generative tools now let anyone rehearse a conversation that has not happened, as many times as they want.

1.Where it began: a conversation I hadn’t had

My parents want me to stay near them. They have rented a flat for me, but I would rather go out and see the world… When I made this work, we had not talked about it yet, so I wanted to rehearse it first.

The method comes from Nathan Fielder’s docuseries The Rehearsal (HBO, 2022). He rebuilds real rooms at full scale in a studio and hires actors, so that his clients can practise a difficult conversation several times before it happens.

3.Where rehearsal is taken seriously

Rehearsing a conversation in advance began as a way for autistic people to handle everyday social life (May, 2022), and as a behavioural exercise in counselling. After The Rehearsal aired, many autistic viewers saw their own daily routine in it (BuzzFeed, 2022). Now ordinary people have started to rehearse with AI before they speak.

2.Questionnaire: what 19 people said

Insight

Data turns what is possible into what is probable. The moments people least want rehearsed are the ones that happen only once.

The questionnaire and the literature point to the same contradiction. People who fear conflict face to face often try a difficult conversation on AI first. But when MIT and OpenAI (2025) followed 981 users, those who leaned most on chatbots for emotional support became lonelier and saw fewer people. Rehearsal was meant to help people speak, but it kept them in the rehearsal room.

The gap: people rehearse again and again before they speak, but still cannot get through the real moment, which comes only once.

Concept

I built a rehearsal room for one, using large language models and a pre-rendered pipeline. A visitor first gives a piece of their own story. A data double then enters the evening of a conversation they have not had. The visitor rewinds and chooses again, then watches the machine run that evening 2,000 times. In the end they see that rehearsal cannot fix a real relationship, and they leave the room to face it.

  • G1 simulate reality build a data double from your handwriting and voice
  • G2 rehearse / replay rehearse a talk you have not had, and replay past moments
  • G3 countless possibilities see the possibilities and dead ends of 2,000 runs
  • G4 back to reality see that rehearsal cannot fix it, and go back to real life, which happens only once

2.Zingaline: four rules taken from a planet

The name comes from Zingaline, a planet in Hao Jingfang’s Invisible Planets. When two people there brush past each other, their bodies overlap and swap a small part. They walk on as new people, but believe they have not changed. I turned this idea into four rules for the room:

  1. Two bodies overlapAct I: your handwriting and a minute of your voice become a data double in the son’s seat.
  2. No one steps in the same river twiceAct III: time only moves forward. Even after a rewind, you are already on a new branch.
  3. New people after partingAct IV: a home video that was never filmed fixes the memory of that evening.
  4. What lingers after the overlapAct V: all 2,000 runs replay. You take the receipt, and your words stay on the computer.

Experiment

3.This evening was rehearsed 2,000 times

Before any visitor arrives, the model has already rehearsed this evening 2,000 times. The runs come in 1,000 pairs. Both runs in a pair use the same random numbers, and only the mother’s first line differs. For the first four turns she has not spoken, so every pair is the same. At turn 5, her move changes in 946 pairs.

This is design goal G3: let people see what 2,000 runs make possible, and where they end up.

But the differences do not last. I designed the son so that his mother’s line does not change him, and at turn 6 every pair is the same again. The father differs in 420 pairs at turn 7. By the end, only 33 of the 1,000 pairs differ, and 664 of the 2,000 runs end in “nothing settled”.

4.One evening, two first lines

Father rented a flat for me to stay in without asking me first. My luggage is already by the door.

  1. aHe spent two months on this flat. At least hear him out.他为这房子跑了两个月，你至少听他说完。she sides with the father · 549 of 1,000
  2. bGo on. Slowly. We’ll listen first.你说。慢一点，我们先听着。she keeps room for the son · 405 of 1,000

both branches end

  1. SonOkay. The key stays here for now.好。钥匙先放这儿。
  2. FatherI’ll cook. We’ll talk about the key tomorrow.饭我做。钥匙的事，明天再说。

Both recorded branches end on the same two lines. The mother’s first line is the one thing the visitor chooses; the shared start runs 24 s, each branch 30.5 s, the rewind 5 s.

05Iteration

Four rounds of prototypes, and how they became the room

Iteration

From June to October I built four rounds of prototypes and changed direction twice. I dropped the research tool in June and froze the phone VR encounters in July.

In August the work ran on two lines: the evening of the last four acts, and the quiz of Act I. After the walkthrough on 13 September I replaced Act I. Four decisions then shaped the room as it is now.

Reflection

4.After building the system

This was the first time I joined computer vision, speech-to-text, a large language model and a 3D state machine into one complete story pipeline. I tried to use engineering to cover every way a family conversation could go. After running 2,000 simulations, I saw more and more clearly that a language model can work out the most likely line, but it does not seem to understand the hesitation and softness of a real relationship. 664 runs ended in “nothing settled”. This showed me where an algorithm stops when it meets a close, complicated relationship. A dozen or so visitors used the dead ends I wrote to rehearse their own regrets. As the person who built the system, I also came to see that rehearsal changes nothing, and I let go of my wish for a “perfect life”. In mid-October, after I took the equipment back from the show, my parents and I did talk about the flat. It went worse than any rehearsal the model made, but somehow… it was better unrehearsed.

这是我第一次把视觉识别、语音转文字、大语言模型和三维状态机串成一条完整的叙事管线，试图用工程的方法去穷举一场家庭对话。在代码里跑完两千次模拟后我愈发意识到，大语言模型即使能算出概率最高的台词，但我们真实关系里的犹豫与心软，它好像真的没办法理解——664 次的结果是“什么都没解决”，也让我看到算法在面对复杂亲密关系时的边界。还有展览里的十几位访客借着我写的死胡同预演了各自的遗憾，作为搭建这套系统的人，最后也看清了排练的徒劳，放下了对“完美人生”的执念。十月中旬把设备从展厅拆回来后，我和爸妈还是聊了租房的事，过程比模型排练出来的还要糟糕，但好像……没有排练更好。
