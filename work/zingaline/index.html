Zingaline

An installation: one visitor at a time, alone at a desk for six to nineteen minutes. You write down one thing you wish you could do over, and the page is shredded. A version of you made from those words takes a son’s place on the night before he leaves home. That night had been rehearsed 2,000 times before you came.

Background

In an age of highly developed social networks and technology, people have become more atomised than ever. It is as if everyone had come down with loneliness, like an illness, one that has to be treated by rehearsing again and again; and AI, as the embodiment of big data, gives people endless chances to rehearse their own lives.

1.Where it came from: a conversation that hasn’t happened yet

My parents want me to rent a place near them; I want to go out and make my own way. This conversation hasn’t happened yet, and I want to rehearse it first.

The idea came from Nathan Fielder’s docu-comedy The Rehearsal (HBO, 2022), in which he helps ordinary people rehearse the big moments of their lives with realistic sets and scripts.

2.The more connected, the more isolated

  • 1 / 6people worldwide are affected by lonelinessWorld Health Organization 2025
  • 64.2%of young people surveyed feel they freeze up socially; 40.3% avoid socialisingChina Youth Daily Social Survey Center 2023 · 2,001 people aged 18–35
  • nearbyXiang Biao’s “disappearance of the nearby”: people have lost the wish, and the ability, to step into the world around them and make sense of itXiang Biao, China News Service 2026

3.Where rehearsal is taken seriously

Autistic people often rehearse situations ahead of time, again and again, to cope with social life that is hard to predict (May, 2022). After the series aired, many autistic viewers said its rehearsals looked like their own daily life (BuzzFeed 2022; OAR 2025).

Insight

Data turns “possible” into “probable”, and what people least want rehearsed are the moments that happen only once.

Two more: the more connected people are, the more isolated they become, and the social moments nobody has rehearsed are handed to AI to practise (see Background); and AI rehearsal can comfort people, but it can also replace people: those who chose to chat more with chatbots were lonelier and saw real people less (MIT and OpenAI 2025, 981 people, 4 weeks; a correlation only).

The gap: between endless rehearsal and a real moment that happens only once

Concept

To use what AI can do to build a rehearsal room that simulates real characters, settings and scenes as closely as possible, where people can rehearse, or re-enact, the important moments that have not happened yet or have already passed; to let them see countless virtual possibilities rehearsed from data; and to let them realise, in the end, that returning to what is real matters most.

  • G1 simulate the real characters, settings and scenes as close to real as possible
  • G2 rehearse / re-enact important moments yet to happen, or already past
  • G3 countless possibilities see the virtual possibilities rehearsed from data
  • G4 back to the real realise that returning to the real matters most

2.Jinjialin: a planet turned into four rules

Zingaline is my spelling of 津加林 (Jinjialin), the last planet in Hao Jingfang’s Invisible Planets. When two of its people walk past each other, their bodies overlap and exchange matter, and they part as two new people who believe they have not changed. In the work, the visitor’s words and the software’s records mix in one figure in the same way. The other source is Heraclitus: “You cannot step into the same river twice.” When the scene goes back in act iii, it fades out and starts again; the second time is not the same evening.

  1. Two bodies overlapAct i: your words and the machine’s records mix into one figure
  2. You cannot step into the same river twiceAct iii: the rewind only fades out and starts again; the second time is not the same evening
  3. Parted, they are new peopleAct iv: an evening that never happened becomes a memory
  4. The composite after the overlapAct v: the choice is no longer yours

2.Why this evening

The scene comes from my own life: my parents want me to rent a place near them, and I want to go out and make my own way. The real conversation hasn’t happened yet, so I wrote it as one evening. It is also the kind of moment people in my questionnaire least wanted rehearsed, people and love; one of them wrote “parting from someone close”.

The parents spent two months finding a flat twenty minutes from the son’s work and have paid the rent; the key is on the table. The son is leaving tomorrow morning as planned, and does not take the key.

Experiment

2.This evening was rehearsed 2,000 times

Before any visitor arrives, the model has rehearsed the evening 2,000 times: 1,000 pairs, each pair run twice with the same random numbers, changing only the mother’s first line.

This is the third part of the design goal (G3): letting people see the many possibilities rehearsed from data.

In the 1,000 pairs the son does the same at every step, and the ending differs in only 33. I designed the son not to be swayed by this line of his mother’s: there are many possibilities, and most of them end in the same place.

3.One evening, two first lines

Father rented a flat for me to stay in without asking me first. My luggage is already by the door.

  1. aHe spent two months on this flat. At least hear him out.他为这房子跑了两个月，你至少听他说完。she sides with the father · 549 of 1,000
  2. bGo on. Slowly. We’ll listen first.你说。慢一点，我们先听着。she keeps room for the son · 405 of 1,000

both branches end

  1. sonOkay. The key stays here for now.好。钥匙先放这儿。
  2. fatherI’ll cook. We’ll talk about the key tomorrow.饭我做。钥匙的事，明天再说。

Both recorded branches end on the same two lines. The mother’s first line is the one thing the visitor chooses; the shared start runs 24 s, each branch 30.5 s, the rewind 5 s.

Reflection

4.After building the system

This was the first time I chained computer vision, voice cloning, a large language model and a 3D state machine into one complete narrative pipeline, trying to exhaust a family conversation by engineering. After running two thousand simulations in code, I came to see more and more that even if a large language model can work out the most probable line, the hesitation and soft-heartedness in our real relationships seem to be something an LLM really cannot understand: 664 runs ending in “nothing resolved” also showed me where algorithms reach their limit in complicated, intimate relationships. And the dozen or so visitors at the show rehearsed their own regrets through the dead end I wrote; as the one who built the system, I too finally saw the futility of rehearsal, and let go of my fixation on a “perfect life”. In mid-October, after I brought the equipment back from the show, my parents and I did talk about renting a place. It went worse than the model’s rehearsals, but somehow… not rehearsing was better.

这是我第一次把视觉识别、语音克隆、大语言模型和三维状态机串成一条完整的叙事管线，试图用工程的方法去穷举一场家庭对话。在代码里跑完两千次模拟后我愈发意识到，大语言模型即使能算出概率最高的台词，但我们真实关系里的犹豫与心软，LLM 好像真的没办法理解——664 次的结果是“什么都没解决”，也让我看到算法在面对复杂亲密关系时的边界。还有展览里的十几位访客借着我写的死胡同预演了各自的遗憾，作为搭建这套系统的人，最后也看清了排练的徒劳，放下了对“完美人生”的执念。十月中旬把设备从展厅拆回来后，我和爸妈还是聊了租房的事，过程比模型彩排出来的还要糟糕，但好像……没有排练更好。
