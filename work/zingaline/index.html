person — data and AI

Zingaline

An installation: one visitor at a time, alone at a desk for six to nineteen minutes. You write down one thing you wish you could do over, and the page is shredded. A version of you made from those words takes a son’s place on the night before he leaves home. That night had been rehearsed 2,000 times before you came.

the project

issue

People are being turned into data that machines can simulate: AI agents built from interviews, AI companions, replicas of the dead. With such tools, a person can rehearse a conversation before having it, or run a past decision again to see how else it could have gone.

position

A version of a person made from data acts on odds. Each time, it draws one of the person’s likely choices, and it does not respond to the people around it. The work argues that what such a version lacks, moments that happen once and can surprise the people in them, is worth keeping.

form

An installation for one visitor at a time. The visitor writes down one thing they wish they could do over, and speaks for a minute about an object on the desk. From these words, the screen draws a figure: the visitor’s data version. In a family scene, the night before a son leaves home, this version takes the son’s place. What the son does was decided in advance: the installation’s software ran the scene 2,000 times before any visit.

aim

To let visitors find out, with their own words, what a rehearsed life leaves out.

the name

Zingaline is my spelling of 津加林 (Jinjialin), the last planet in Hao Jingfang’s Invisible Planets. When two of its people walk past each other, their bodies overlap and exchange matter, and they part as two new people who believe they have not changed. In the work, the visitor’s words and the software’s records mix in one figure in the same way. The other source is Heraclitus: “You cannot step into the same river twice.” When the scene goes back in act iii, it fades out and starts again; the second time is not the same evening.

01Research

would you run it again?

Before making the work, I asked 19 people: if you could run a decision in your life many times to see the different outcomes, would you? Their answers, and how people are already rebuilt as data, set the work’s position.

2.1 AI already rebuilds people from their data

Six recent figures show the situation the work responds to. One mark is one person, one percentage point or $100 m.

2.2 7 of the 19 would run a decision again, yet find an unrehearsed life more interesting

13 of the 19 would run a life decision many times. 11 say a life that cannot be rehearsed is more interesting. 7 say both, and the work is made for them.

2.3 My reasoning, from these answers to the work’s position

This is my argument; the survey does not prove it. The last step decides how the work ends.

  1. dataa life is easy to turn into data and simulate
  2. oddsa version made of data draws its choices from odds
  3. no responseit does not respond to others, so nothing can surprise it
  4. carried throughthe work runs the simulation to the end, to show what it leaves out
  5. one momentthe work ends with the visit itself, on a black screen that says “This time, there was no rehearsal.”

03Making

2,000 runs of one scene: the mother’s first line never changes what the son does

In this model the son’s choices do not depend on what his mother says; I designed him that way, and the runs show what follows. Each pair is one run made twice with the same random numbers, once with each of her two first lines. In all 1,000 pairs the son does the same, and the ending changes in only 33: the visitor’s choice in act iii almost never changes how the evening ends.

One evening, two first lines

Father rented a flat for me to stay in without asking me first. My luggage is already by the door.

  1. aHe spent two months on this flat. At least hear him out.他为这房子跑了两个月，你至少听他说完。she sides with the father · 549 of 1,000
  2. bGo on. Slowly. We’ll listen first.你说。慢一点，我们先听着。she keeps room for the son · 405 of 1,000

both branches end

  1. sonOkay. The key stays here for now.好。钥匙先放这儿。
  2. fatherI’ll cook. We’ll talk about the key tomorrow.饭我做。钥匙的事，明天再说。

Both recorded branches end on the same two lines. The mother’s first line is the one thing the visitor chooses; the shared start runs 24 s, each branch 30.5 s, the rewind 5 s.

What this record can't say

  • The room is an exhibition concept drawn as a plan. Nothing is built, the sizes are assumed, and no visitor has sat at the desk. The camera gesture, the shredder trigger and the printer have not run on real hardware; the desk test used a synthetic page and a fake webcam.
  • The 19 answers come from people I know, self-reported in Chinese and translated by me. They set the work’s position; they do not prove its argument, and the page says so where they appear.
  • The 2,000 runs are frequencies inside a model I wrote. The son was designed not to depend on his mother’s line, so how little the fork changes is a property of the model, not a finding about families. The work says “frequency”, never “probability”.
  • The home video shows an evening nobody filmed: it was generated from the rehearsal as reference, and the app and the pages label it as such. The bodies were dressed again because bare clay was refused as nudity by the generator.
  • One step can go online, to find the events in the visitor’s words, and only with their consent; everything else runs on the one computer. The receipt prints counts and labels, never the visitor’s sentences.

AI use

Parts of the code were written with AI coding assistants to my specifications, and AI assisted the data analysis. The twelve clips of the home video were generated with MiniMax H3 from the stage as reference, and some 3D assets come from Tripo AI, checked before use. Inside the work, DeepSeek V4 reads the visitor’s page, in live mode only and after consent.
